Pillar-0 Overview

We introduce Pillar-0, a radiology foundation model pretrained on 143k CTs and 11k MRIs from a large academic center, together with RATE, a scalable framework that extracts structured labels from radiology reports for 366 clinically grounded findings with near-perfect accuracy.

Pillar-0 establishes a new performance frontier across multiple benchmarks and tasks:

  • Internal RATE-Evals: Outperforms MedGemma, MedImageInsight, Lingshu, and Merlin by 7.8–15.8 AUC points, ranking best in 87.2% of tasks across all modalities.
  • External validation: Outperforms all baselines on the Stanford abdomen-pelvis CT dataset, including Merlin (82.2 vs 80.6 AUC), which uses the Stanford dataset for development.
  • Lung cancer risk prediction: Sets new state-of-the-art by improving upon Sybil by 3.0 C-index points on NLST, with strong generalization to MGH (+5.9) and CGMH (+1.9).
  • Data efficiency: Achieves >95 AUC in brain hemorrhage detection using only 5% of the data required by the next most sample-efficient baseline—20–40× fewer labels.

Pillar-0 and RATE together provide an open, clinically rigorous foundation for building high-performance radiology systems, enabling applications that were previously infeasible due to computational, data, and evaluation constraints.

Pillar-0 key innovations

(A) Modality-specific multi-windowing converts full-resolution CT and MRI volumes into multi-channel inputs that emulate radiologist workflow presets, preserving clinically relevant contrast (+4.6 AUROC point gain).

(B) The Atlas vision backbone efficiently processes long-context volumes, 175x faster than ViT-S, achieving state-of-the-art performance with fewer parameters than other medical foundation models.

(C) Asymmetric contrastive pretraining aligns Atlas volume embeddings with embeddings from a larger LLM text encoder for a much stronger correlation between loss and downstream performance.

Clinically Grounded Evaluation

We introduce RATE, a unified framework designed to evaluate any vision model on full-fidelity medical volumes, using authentic clinical tasks derived from real-world radiology practice. Board-certified radiologists curated 366 diverse radiologic findings reflecting real-world practice across our modalities. RATE then uses open large language models to extract binary labels for each task from radiology reports, enabling scalable and clinically grounded benchmarking. Building on this framework, we introduce RATE-Evals, a standardized protocol for assessing pretrained vision encoders on real-world medical imaging datasets using linear probing.

Results

Internal RATE-Evals. AUROC: 86.4 (abdomen-pelvis), 88.0 (chest), 90.1 (head), 82.9 (breast); +7.8–15.8 vs. strongest baseline. Wins 319/366 findings (87.2%), ≥84.3% per modality.

External. Stanford Merlin abdomen-pelvis CT: 82.2 AUROC vs. Merlin 80.6. Stanford-only Pillar-0 also 82.2; Pillar-0 init + Stanford finetune reaches 84.9 (Table 1).

Lung cancer risk. Sybil-1.5 (Pillar-0 finetuned) lifts 1–6 year AUROC on NLST, MGH, and CGMH while localizing suspicious regions from a single LDCT.

Data efficiency. RSNA-2019 ICH: AUROC 95.0 with 2.5% data; Swin3D-t needs 50%, Merlin 75%, 3D ResNet 100%—20–40× fewer labels.
