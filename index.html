Abstract

Pillar-0 is a radiology foundation model trained on 218k CT and MRI exams (155k for pretraining, 31k for internal evaluation) with 366 radiologist-curated findings. The model preserves native volumetric resolution via modality-aware tokenization, leverages a multi-scale Atlas backbone for 3D efficiency, and aligns images to large text encoders through asymmetric contrastive learning. Paired with the RATE evaluation framework, Pillar-0 delivers state-of-the-art AUROC on abdomen-pelvis CT, chest CT, head CT, and breast MRI, outperforming MedGemma, MedImageInsight, Lingshu, and Merlin while enabling data-efficient downstream finetuning.

Pillar-0 Overview

Volumetric-first foundation model trained on 155k CT/MR exams and evaluated on 31k more across 366 radiologist-curated findings. Keeps native resolution and radiologist viewing presets, winning 87.2% of RATE findings and leading AUROC on abdomen-pelvis CT, chest CT, head CT, and breast MRI.

RATE extracts labels from real reports; RATE-Evals freezes the encoder and trains one linear probe per finding to measure representation quality on full-resolution volumes.

Core Innovation

Volumetric Encoder Design (Figure 2)

Modality-aware multi-window tokenization mirrors radiologist presets and preserves subtle contrast (+4.6 AUROC on abdomen-pelvis CT). The Atlas multi-scale backbone replaces quadratic attention with $O(N \log N)$ attention, running 175× faster than ViT-S on $384^3$ inputs while keeping native 3D reasoning. Asymmetric contrastive pretraining aligns Atlas with a frozen LLM text encoder, making pretraining loss a reliable signal for downstream clinical performance.

Figure 2. Multi-window tokenization, Atlas multi-scale attention, and asymmetric contrastive pretraining drive Pillar-0 performance.

Clinically Grounded Evaluation (Figure 3)

RATE is the only benchmark using full-resolution volumes, clinically authored findings, and report-derived labels instead of synthetic or textbook snapshots. RATE-Evals trains one linear probe per task on frozen encoders, enabling fair comparisons across vision models and modalities.

Figure 3. RATE transforms radiology reports into scalable, clinically grounded labels and standardizes linear-probe evaluation.

Results

Internal RATE-Evals. AUROC: 86.4 (abdomen-pelvis), 88.0 (chest), 90.1 (head), 82.9 (breast); +7.8–15.8 vs. strongest baseline. Wins 319/366 findings (87.2%), ≥84.3% per modality.

External. Stanford Merlin abdomen-pelvis CT: 82.2 AUROC vs. Merlin 80.6. Stanford-only Pillar-0 also 82.2; Pillar-0 init + Stanford finetune reaches 84.9 (Table 1).

Lung cancer risk. Sybil-1.5 (Pillar-0 finetuned) lifts 1–6 year AUROC on NLST, MGH, and CGMH while localizing suspicious regions from a single LDCT.

Data efficiency. RSNA-2019 ICH: AUROC 95.0 with 2.5% data; Swin3D-t needs 50%, Merlin 75%, 3D ResNet 100%—20–40× fewer labels.
